    </h1>
  </div>

  <p class="font-bold text-xl sm:text-2xl text-neutral-900 dark:text-white tracking-tight">
    I am going to suffer writing this.
  </p>

  <p>
    Genuine suffering mixed with dread, because long before I became a pundit or data analyst, I am first and foremost a fan.
  </p>

  <p>
    A fan tasked with breaking down his biggest, most bitter rival. The rival we defeated precisely once via a solitary goal from a striker who isn't even here anymore (shoutout Martha Thomas). A fan who, virtually every single season, has to endure at least one 5-0 hammering in the derby. And if that isn't torture enough, as an England supporter, half of the very players humiliating my club on weekends handed me some of the greatest sporting highs of my life during the Euros.
  </p>

  <div class="border-l-2 border-neutral-400 dark:border-neutral-600 pl-4 my-4 py-1 text-neutral-700 dark:text-neutral-300 italic text-base">
    It is hatred tangled with fear, admiration, and pure despair.
  </div>

  <p>
    Despair watching <strong>Alessia Russo</strong> execute a devastating high press, nick the ball off our reigning player of the season, and stroll in on goal. Despair watching <strong>Lotte Wubben-Moy</strong> casually sweep up every single counter-attack. And despair surveying the terrifying caliber of Arsenal's summer recruitment.
  </p>

  <p>
    My first team breakdown had to focus on London City Lionesses because of the unprecedented narrative and media frenzy, but Arsenal conducted the sharpest, most surgical business in the transfer market. Dissecting it is painful.
  </p>

  <p>
    Fortunately, as an analyst, the numbers are impartial. The data will keep me honest and objective.
  </p>

  <p class="font-semibold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white">
    I'll finish with a wish: may something unspool on the red side of North London this season. But looking at the squad sheet... it doesn't look promising for the rest of us. Not even a little.
  </p>

  <hr class="border-neutral-200 dark:border-neutral-800 my-6">

  <p>
    Arsenal is the second-biggest juggernaut in the division. An institution that has produced world-beating sides, lifted European silverware, and routinely packs the Emirates with 30,000 to 50,000 fans.
  </p>

  <p>
    The demand for a league title has ceased to be an ambition; it is now an uncompromising prerequisite. Throughout this analytical series, we'll examine their tactical setup, new additions, and confront the age-old question: <em>"Does Arsenal truly suffer from an opening-month hangover, and can the rest of the league capitalize?"</em>
  </p>
