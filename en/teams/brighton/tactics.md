  <div class="space-y-4">
    <h2 class="text-xl font-bold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white">
      Two Different Tactical Worlds
    </h2>

    <p>
      Under Dario Vidošić, Brighton’s identity was unmistakable: pure positional play (<em>Juego de Posición</em>), methodical, patient, and uncompromisingly measured. Vidošić encapsulated this in an interview with <em>The Halfway Line</em> with a quote that defined his tenure: <strong class="text-neutral-900 dark:text-white">"When we don't have the ball, I suffer."</strong> His system (predominantly 3-4-2-1 or an asymmetrical diamond) was constructed around short, calculated progression from the goalkeeper, manufacturing central overloads in the half-spaces, and maintaining a narrow, suffocating rest-defense to suppress opponent transitions.
    </p>

    <p>
      For high-football-IQ playmakers, it was an ideal ecosystem. In an in-depth feature with <em>The Guardian</em>, Fran Kirby explained exactly why Vidošić’s framework elevated her at age 32:
    </p>

    <div class="rounded-xl border border-neutral-200 dark:border-neutral-800 bg-neutral-50 dark:bg-[#121215] p-5 my-2">
      <div class="text-xs font-mono text-neutral-500 dark:text-neutral-400 font-bold uppercase mb-1">
        Fran Kirby · via The Guardian
      </div>
      <p class="text-base sm:text-lg italic text-neutral-800 dark:text-neutral-200 font-serif">
        "Instead of running around like a headless chicken, I stand and assess. The system allows me to use my brain and find passes other people won't try."
      </p>
    </div>

    <p>
      Then came August 2026: Vidošić departed for Angel City, and Arturo Ruiz arrived from Real Sociedad. Ruiz brought an entirely different Spanish school of thought: not patient circulation, but aggressive space exploitation, high-intensity forward pressing (known in Spain as <em>"Morder"</em> — biting into the opposition build-up), and rapid vertical directness into the channels.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="space-y-4 pt-2">
    <h2 class="text-xl font-bold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white">
      The Human Factor & Analytical Takeaway
    </h2>

    <p>
      On her podcast <em>'Added Time'</em>, Fran Kirby connected the tactical reality to the dressing room: <strong class="text-neutral-900 dark:text-white">"The managers' styles are different, and we are still trying, together with me, to learn and see what we can do."</strong>
    </p>

    <p>
      Transitioning to high-pressing verticality forces players to execute at a 180-bpm heart rate. Ruiz's aggressive counter-pressing wins the ball back high and unleashes a flood of chances (8.33 xG in 4 weeks!), but finishing composure inside the box inevitably lags during the early learning curve.
    </p>

    <p>
      In modern analytics, a single match scoreline can be deceptive, but underlying chance generation rarely is. A team sustaining 2.08 xG and 34 box touches per match will see its results revert toward the mean once conversion rates normalize. Brighton under Arturo Ruiz are not a broken side; they are a high-powered creation engine primed for a sharp upward correction.
    </p>
