    Analyzing Brighton almost feels improper.
  </p>

  <p>
    Why improper? Because this is an organization that lives and breathes quantitative analytics. They are an ultra-advanced football club employing sophisticated, finely-tuned proprietary models and algorithmic recruitment systems — so what could I possibly add from my outside laptop perspective?
  </p>

  <p class="font-semibold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white">
    I genuinely don't know.
  </p>

  <div class="border-l-2 border-neutral-400 dark:border-neutral-600 pl-4 my-4 py-1 text-neutral-700 dark:text-neutral-300 italic text-base">
    Yet how exhilarating is it that data analytics actually dictates the agenda across this league? How refreshing that a club doesn't just mention data in press releases, but builds an entire strategic foundation on it. It is an incredible challenge, and hopefully the insights here can capture at least a fraction of the depth they build inside.
  </div>

  <div class="space-y-4 pt-2">
    <h2 class="text-xl font-bold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white">
      The August Earthquake: From Vidošić to Ruiz
    </h2>

    <p>
      And then came the summer. Just days before the opening whistle of the 2026/27 campaign, <strong>Dario Vidošić</strong> departed Brighton in a shock move to take over Angel City in the NWSL. Such an abrupt exit, a mere week before Matchweek 1, completely disrupted the pre-season preparation and tactical continuity the squad had worked on.
    </p>

    <p>
      In response, Brighton moved swiftly, triggering the buyout clause to appoint <strong>Arturo Ruiz</strong> — a promising 29-year-old Spanish head coach who had already guided both Atlético Madrid and Real Sociedad to UEFA Women's Champions League qualification spots.
    </p>

    <p>
      The differences between the two tacticians are stark: in setup, philosophy, and in-game tempo. It is fascinating that a purely data-driven club, which under Vidošić cultivated patient, deliberate positional play, chose a coach whose identity is built on vertical transitions, aggressive pressing, and explosive tempo. Today, four rounds into the season, it is clear that the squad's adaptation to these new demands is anything but trivial.
    </p>

    <h2 class="text-xl font-bold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white pt-2">
      The Table's Illusion vs. Underlying Reality
    </h2>

    <p>
      And the early result on the pitch? A historical statistical anomaly.
    </p>

    <p>
      Look only at the raw standings, and Brighton sit 11th with just 2 points and zero wins from 4 matches. It would be tempting to write this off as a managerial crisis.
    </p>

    <p>
      The underlying numbers, however, tell the exact opposite story: this is a side generating <strong>8.33 Expected Goals (xG)</strong> across 4 rounds (a top-three creation rate in the WSL!), firing 61 shots, recording 135 touches in the opposition penalty area, and missing no fewer than 10 Big Chances — converting all of that into just 3 actual goals. A staggering deficit of more than 5 goals below expected output.
    </p>
