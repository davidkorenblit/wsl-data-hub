    <h1 class="text-2xl md:text-3xl font-bold tracking-tight text-neutral-900 dark:text-white">
      Tottenham Hotspur: My Love, My Club
    </h1>
  </div>

  <p class="font-bold text-xl sm:text-2xl text-neutral-900 dark:text-white tracking-tight">
    Spurs. Tottenham Hotspur. My love, my club.
  </p>

  <p>
    Where do you even begin? I could tell you about that old post back when I still had Instagram, chatting with analyst Oshrat Eini to break down Beth England's blockbuster arrival. I could tell you about falling completely in love with Dele back in the day, which is the whole reason I became a Spurs supporter in the first place. Or we can talk about the fear lurking in the back of almost every fan's mind.
  </p>

  <p>
    The classic "second-season syndrome." The last manager who fell victim to it here was Rehanne. The first year was electric; towards the tail end things started coming undone, and then year two turned into a full-on nightmare — no defensive cohesion, a missing midfield, barely an attack. Could that happen under Robert Vilahamn?
  </p>

  <div class="border-l-2 border-neutral-400 dark:border-neutral-600 pl-4 my-4 py-1 text-neutral-700 dark:text-neutral-300 italic text-base">
    There are good reasons why it won't: First, this team didn't collapse at almost any stage last season. Second, the financial backing and genuine responsibility Robert takes on. Third, the competitive atmosphere across the league that simply won't let Spurs fall apart so easily.
  </div>

  <p>
    We'll get to the transfer business, departures, tactical setups, and data-driven projections soon enough. But before anything else: what a relief it is to finally see serious investment going into this squad.
  </p>

  <hr class="border-neutral-200 dark:border-neutral-800 my-6">

  <h2 class="text-xl font-bold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white">
    The Leadership Void & The Chelsea Connection
  </h2>

  <p>
    This summer saw both <strong>Beth England</strong> and <strong>Molly Bartrip</strong> depart.
    Molly went on <em>Upfront</em> and opened up about her journey and how much Beth helped her along the way — so it's no surprise they both made the move to Crystal Palace together. Their departure left an enormous leadership vacuum. Beth's farewell videos captured her story perfectly: an under-appreciated player who arrived with something to prove, and steadily grew into the heart and soul of this dressing room.
  </p>

  <p>
    This window, another player arrived from across London with a point to prove after a challenging spell, ready to fill that exact void.
  </p>

  <p class="font-semibold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white">
    I'll make a bet right now: next season, <strong>Victoria Pelova</strong> will be wearing the captain's armband. Top player, top mentality.
  </p>

  <hr class="border-neutral-200 dark:border-neutral-800 my-6">

  <h2 class="text-xl font-bold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white">
    Matchweek 1 Reality Check: West Ham (H)
  </h2>

  <p>
    What a way to kick off the season. Signe Gaupset was simply sensational — scoring in the 18th minute and providing the assist for the second at the half-hour mark. It's already clear that Pelova and Gaupset are rotating roles rather than crowding the same space.
  </p>

  <p>
    Defensively, conceding late was frustrating, but Kristi Hansen demonstrated why she is so dangerous: scoring the routine chance on a fast break, and converting from almost nothing (xG 0.04) on the second. Crucially, Spurs produced more than double the attacking threat of West Ham. The fluidity in the final third is real.
  </p>
