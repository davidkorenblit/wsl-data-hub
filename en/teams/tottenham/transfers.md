  <div class="space-y-4">
    <p class="text-lg">
      In <a href="{{ '/en/teams/tottenham/analysis/' | relative_url }}" class="text-neutral-900 dark:text-white underline font-semibold">Part II</a>, we examined how Tottenham's attack last season was anchored in positionless principles: 
      <strong>35 league goals distributed across 11 different scorers</strong>, 58.6% of penalty-box touches originating from non-traditional number nines, and a remarkable 74.2% spatial overlap between attacking midfielders and forwards.
    </p>

    <p>
      Yet that structure was still somewhat constrained: the presence of <strong>Beth England</strong> inevitably tethered the side to a static penalty-box focal point, while <strong>Signe Gaupset</strong> was burdened with deep ball progression and <strong>Olivia Holdt</strong> burned precious physical reserves tracking touchlines.
    </p>

    <div class="p-4 rounded-lg bg-neutral-100 dark:bg-neutral-800/60 border border-neutral-200 dark:border-neutral-700 text-neutral-800 dark:text-neutral-200 text-sm sm:text-base font-medium">
      💡 <strong>Core Hypothesis:</strong> England's departure and the arrivals of <strong>Victoria Pelova, Kirsty Hanson, and Shakira Martinez</strong> do not represent mere personnel swaps. They signify an evolution into <strong>Positionless 2.0</strong>: a basketball-style offensive system where space is constantly reimagined, pressure is shared, and opposing backlines are deprived of a singular target to mark.
    </div>
  </div>

  <!-- Section 1: The Departure of Beth England -->
  <div class="rounded-xl border border-neutral-200 dark:border-neutral-800 bg-neutral-50/50 dark:bg-[#121215] p-5 sm:p-6 space-y-4">
    <h2 class="text-xl font-bold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white flex items-center gap-2">
      <span>1. The End of the Classical #9: Why England's Exit Unlocks Space</span>
    </h2>

    <p class="text-sm sm:text-base text-neutral-700 dark:text-neutral-300">
      For two and a half years, Beth England was the heartbeat of Tottenham. She notched 7 goals in 1,326 minutes last season (0.48 per 90) and was the consummate penalty-box presence.
      Yet in Vilahamn's fluid brand of football, an orthodox #9 inadvertently imposes a tactical ceiling:
    </p>

    <ul class="list-disc list-inside space-y-2 text-sm sm:text-base pl-2 text-neutral-700 dark:text-neutral-300">
      <li><strong>Predictable reference point for center-backs:</strong> A stationary focal point allows opponent central defenders to maintain central compactness without ever being dragged into wide or uncomfortable zones.</li>
      <li><strong>Congesting second-line penetrations:</strong> Constant box occupancy restricted the vacated corridors needed for Gaupset and Holdt to burst into the area with dynamic off-ball runs.</li>
    </ul>

    <p class="text-sm sm:text-base text-neutral-700 dark:text-neutral-300">
      Rather than replacing like-for-like, Vilahamn executed a calculated shift: retaining <strong>Cathinka Tandberg</strong> as a specialized tactical battering ram against deep defensive blocks (98th percentile in aerial duels won with 3.73 per 90), while configuring the remainder of his front line into a fast, positionless trio.
    </p>
  </div>

    <div class="space-y-4 pt-2 text-sm sm:text-base text-neutral-700 dark:text-neutral-300">
      
      <!-- Hanson -->
      <div class="p-4 rounded-lg bg-white dark:bg-[#18181c] border border-neutral-200 dark:border-neutral-800 space-y-2">
        <h3 class="font-bold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white text-base flex items-center justify-between">
          <span>A. Kirsty Hanson: Striker Finishing from Wide Angles & Releasing Holdt</span>
          <span class="text-xs font-mono px-2 py-0.5 rounded bg-emerald-500/10 text-emerald-600 font-bold">12 GOALS · 131% FINISHING EFFICIENCY</span>
        </h3>
        <p>
          Last season, Olivia Holdt spent <strong>26.2% of her match actions glued to the left touchline</strong>. That thankless running dragged her away from high-probability scoring zones.
        </p>
        <p>
          Hanson's arrival from Aston Villa reconfigures that dynamic entirely: Hanson delivers <strong>6.26 box touches per 90</strong> (higher than most standard #9s in the WSL) and ruthless finishing — generating an <strong>xGOT of 8.80 against just 6.71 xG</strong>.
          With Hanson driving inward from the left flank, <strong>Holdt is liberated to operate centrally</strong> as a lethal second striker.
        </p>
      </div>

      <!-- Martinez -->
      <div class="p-4 rounded-lg bg-white dark:bg-[#18181c] border border-neutral-200 dark:border-neutral-800 space-y-2">
        <h3 class="font-bold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white text-base flex items-center justify-between">
          <span>B. Shakira Martinez: Fluidity Incarnate & Right Half-Space Occupation</span>
          <span class="text-xs font-mono px-2 py-0.5 rounded bg-blue-500/10 text-blue-600 font-bold">VERSATILITY: 15 ST / 4 RW / 2 AM</span>
        </h3>
        <p>
          While Tandberg spent <strong>65.8% of her match minutes anchored deep in the penalty area</strong> (a fluidity score of just 44), Shakira Martinez offers the exact antithesis: at West Ham she started across three separate tactical roles.
        </p>
        <p>
          Martinez's lateral movement disorients compact defensive setups. She naturally drifts into the <strong>right half-space</strong> — previously Tottenham's most barren attacking corridor (where Gaupset accounted for only 17.5% of actions) — providing crucial balance to a previously lopsided attack.
        </p>
      </div>

      <!-- Pelova -->
      <div class="p-4 rounded-lg bg-white dark:bg-[#18181c] border border-neutral-200 dark:border-neutral-800 space-y-2">
        <h3 class="font-bold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white text-base flex items-center justify-between">
          <span>C. Victoria Pelova: Unlocking the Signe Gaupset Bottleneck</span>
          <span class="text-xs font-mono px-2 py-0.5 rounded bg-purple-500/10 text-purple-600 font-bold">94th PCT CREATION · 100th PCT LONG BALLS</span>
        </h3>
        <p>
          Tottenham's primary vulnerability down the stretch was overload: whenever opposing managers marked Gaupset tightly (74.56 touches per 90), Tottenham's progression stalled. Gaupset was forced to drop down to her center-backs just to retrieve possession.
        </p>
        <p>
          Pelova's signing from Arsenal injects an elite tempo conductor: <strong>2.38 chances created per 90 (94th percentile)</strong>, <strong>0.27 xA</strong>, and an unmatched <strong>78.3% long-range passing accuracy</strong>. Pelova dictates buildup, allowing Gaupset to return to what she does best: registering 5+ box touches and operating as a devastating scoring weapon.
        </p>
      </div>
