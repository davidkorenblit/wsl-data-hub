  <div class="space-y-4">
    <p class="text-lg">
      A cold look at last season's table illustrates why Chelsea carries such a massive burden of proof: <strong>a 3rd-place finish</strong>, relinquishing the crown to Arsenal, and an early European exit.
    </p>

    <p>
      The attack still found the net 44 times, but beneath that surface, the underlying metrics reveal the true driver of their decline: <strong>Chelsea's backline was historically exposed</strong>.
      The side conceded 20 goals — but according to Expected Goals Against (xGA, measuring the quality of chances surrendered), they should have conceded <strong>26.9 goals!</strong>
    </p>

    <div class="p-4 rounded-lg bg-amber-500/10 border border-amber-500/30 text-amber-900 dark:text-amber-300 text-sm sm:text-base font-medium">
      💡 <strong>The Bottom Line:</strong> Only a career season between the posts by Hannah Hampton (preventing nearly 7 clear goals) kept this backline from looking like a mid-table unit.
    </div>

    <p>
      This drop-off was not born of individual technical regression, but rather a <strong>distinct tactical paradox</strong> inherent to Sonia Bompastor's philosophy — a pattern tracing directly back to her tenure at Olympique Lyonnais.
    </p>
  </div>

  <!-- Section 1: The Bompastor Paradox -->
  <div class="rounded-xl border border-neutral-200 dark:border-neutral-800 bg-neutral-50/50 dark:bg-[#121215] p-5 sm:p-6 space-y-4">
    <h2 class="text-xl font-bold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white flex items-center gap-2">
      <span>1. The Structural Paradox: Demanding Recovery Pace with Unsuited Profiles</span>
    </h2>

    <p class="text-sm sm:text-base text-neutral-700 dark:text-neutral-300">
      Bompastor's tactical identity hinges on aggressive final-third overloads. In possession, Chelsea transitions into an extreme 3-2-5 structure:
    </p>

    <ul class="list-disc list-inside space-y-2 text-sm sm:text-base pl-2 text-neutral-700 dark:text-neutral-300">
      <li><strong>Ellie Carpenter</strong> (logging 1,632 defensive minutes — highest among outfield defenders) bombs down the entire right touchline to the corner flag.</li>
      <li>On the left, an attacking winger like <strong>Sandy Baltimore</strong> drives inward as an additional forward.</li>
      <li>Behind them remains an isolated back three tasked with defending 68 meters of lateral pitch width on a hyper-aggressive high line (45 meters from goal).</li>
    </ul>

    <p class="text-sm sm:text-base text-neutral-700 dark:text-neutral-300">
      For such a setup to survive direct transitions, you require athletic center-backs with exceptional <em>recovery pace</em> capable of turning and tracking deep balls.
      In practice, with <strong>Millie Bright</strong> and <strong>Niamh Charles</strong> sidelined for major stretches and <strong>Kadeisha Buchanan</strong> starting just 3 matches, Chelsea's defensive profile looked like this:
    </p>
