      Manchester City: Champions, Ambition & Football That's Pure Joy to Watch
    </h1>
  </div>

  <p class="font-bold text-xl sm:text-2xl text-neutral-900 dark:text-white tracking-tight">
    On Manchester City.
  </p>

  <p>
    City won the league because they were the best team in the country, by far.<br>
    City is a fascinating club to evaluate: throughout the Covid era and before, they fielded absurdly talented squads (Keira Walsh, Jill Scott, and Sam Mewis in midfield, Lucy Bronze, and endless others). Look for virtually any world-class English veteran over the past decade, and chances are she spent peak years at City lifting silverware.
  </p>

  <p>
    Last season was simply a treat to watch. Vivianne Miedema, Hempo (Lauren Hemp), and Khadija "Bunny" Shaw put on the real show of the division.
  </p>

  <p class="font-semibold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white">
    In the transfer market, adding Beth Mead and Niamh Charles makes a massive statement, though the true crowning achievement of the summer was securing Bunny Shaw to a long-term extension.
  </p>

  <div class="border-l-2 border-neutral-400 dark:border-neutral-600 pl-4 my-4 py-1 text-neutral-700 dark:text-neutral-300 italic text-base">
    Beyond budgets and wages, keeping Shaw is an emphatic statement of intent: the premier striker in world football stays put. That is Chelsea-esque behavior in the most complimentary sense — no key star leaves here in her prime.
  </div>

  <p>
    City have firmly consolidated their place at the European summit alongside Arsenal and Chelsea, knocking right on the door of Barcelona and Lyon. And their style of play is so fluid, expansive, and aesthetically pleasing that they are comfortably the most enjoyable neutral watch in the league (Spurs can never hold that title for me, because as a supporter, you are always in a state of nervous anxiety. Always.)
  </p>
