      <p class="text-lg sm:text-xl font-medium text-neutral-900 dark:text-white">
        Matchweek 1 has come and gone, and here are a few things that caught my eye.
      </p>

      <hr class="border-neutral-200 dark:border-neutral-800 my-4">

      <!-- Tottenham -->
      <section class="space-y-3">
        <h2 class="text-xl sm:text-2xl font-bold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white flex items-center gap-2">
          <span>⚪</span>
          <span>Starting with Spurs</span>
        </h2>
        <p>It was fun. Honestly, what else can you say? It was just pure fun.</p>
        <p>
          Signe Gaupset was unreal — finding the net in the 18th minute and providing the assist for the second on the half-hour mark, all in the same game. Interestingly, it seems Pelova is currently her direct rotation rather than them sharing the pitch together. Alys was out injured and Dykstra operated on the right flank, which was a very intriguing wrinkle.
        </p>
        <p>
          Conceding at the death was frustrating, but Kristi Hansen showed exactly what she's about: <strong class="text-neutral-900 dark:text-white">scoring the routine chance</strong> (Fast Break, 30') and <strong class="text-neutral-900 dark:text-white">converting out of pure thin air</strong> (Regular Play, 74', an xG of just 0.04 — basically nothing, and still nestled in the net). Two completely different types of goals, both clinical. Also, credit to Tannberg for a lovely knockdown on the 2-0.
        </p>
        <p class="text-sm text-neutral-500 dark:text-neutral-400 font-mono">
          In terms of control — Spurs created more than double the genuine chances West Ham managed to muster.
        </p>
      </section>

      <hr class="border-neutral-200 dark:border-neutral-800 my-4">

      <!-- Arsenal -->
      <section class="space-y-3">
        <h2 class="text-xl sm:text-2xl font-bold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white flex items-center gap-2">
          <span>🔴</span>
          <span>Arsenal: The Eye-Test Deception</span>
        </h2>
        <p>
          I only managed to catch the closing stages because both games were kicking off simultaneously, and the heavy legs were obvious. But the numbers tell an entirely different story: <strong class="text-neutral-900 dark:text-white">Arsenal actually dominated the second half</strong>, creating significantly more threat than Brighton and doing what you'd expect: keeping the door shut and threatening dangerously. What the naked eye saw late on was fatigue; what the data saw was territorial control.
        </p>
        <p>
          And then Brighton didn't even need prolonged pressure, because <strong class="text-neutral-900 dark:text-white">Lisa Baum</strong> hit a sensational strike. A player clearly operating a level above, unbothered by the fact that Arsenal usually starts the season slow.
        </p>
      </section>

      <hr class="border-neutral-200 dark:border-neutral-800 my-4">

      <!-- Chelsea -->
      <section class="space-y-3">
        <h2 class="text-xl sm:text-2xl font-bold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white flex items-center gap-2">
          <span>🔵</span>
          <span>Chelsea</span>
        </h2>
        <p>
          Looked almost identical to last season. A shape I couldn't quite decipher, neither out of possession nor going forward — but they created chances in bundles. <strong class="text-neutral-900 dark:text-white">38 total chances created</strong> to Villa's 5; an xG of 3.04 against 0.43. Calling it "endless opportunities" isn't an exaggeration.
        </p>
        <p>
          Lauren James with a piece of magic in the 11th minute, left boot from inside the box. And then they conceded... and once we verified the data, it all made sense: <strong class="text-neutral-900 dark:text-white">it wasn't even a Villa player</strong>. Veerle Buurman of Chelsea put it into her own net in the 79th minute. An own goal. That player chasing back desperately? It was a Blue. That explains the bizarre visual.
        </p>
      </section>

      <hr class="border-neutral-200 dark:border-neutral-800 my-4">

      <!-- Goalkeepers -->
      <section class="space-y-3">
        <h2 class="text-xl sm:text-2xl font-bold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white flex items-center gap-2">
          <span>🧤</span>
          <span>Between the Sticks</span>
        </h2>
        <p>
          Aston Villa's keeper put on an absolute clinic. Surprising to see Misa Rodríguez and Elena start ahead of Daphne van Domselaar (possible knock?) and Mary Earps, and honestly, I'd be very happy to see Lize Kop rotated to the bench.
        </p>
      </section>

      <hr class="border-neutral-200 dark:border-neutral-800 my-4">

      <!-- Promoted / New Arrivals -->
      <section class="space-y-3">
        <h2 class="text-xl sm:text-2xl font-bold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white flex items-center gap-2">
          <span>🦅</span>
          <span>The New Arrivals & Crystal Palace</span>
        </h2>
        <p>
          Palace — from the highlights — looked energetic and hungry, but not quite in sync just yet. A 1-0 win over Everton, where Everton actually racked up more shot volume — a victory born out of clinical efficiency rather than dominance.
        </p>
        <p>
          Beth England didn't find her groove — 3 attempts, none hitting the target. Molly Bartrip, however, was rock solid (and yes, bias noted!). 7 tackles, 12 defensive actions, a 7.61 FotMob rating. Steady as a rock.
        </p>
        <p>
          Birmingham City proved no match for a flying Liverpool. That second Liverpool goal was absurd — every center-back's lifelong dream. The kind of volley you only catch like that when you're playing DLS on your phone. It'll be very interesting to see what Martin Taylor does there.
        </p>
      </section>

      <hr class="border-neutral-200 dark:border-neutral-800 my-4">

      <!-- Manchester City -->
      <section class="space-y-3">
        <h2 class="text-xl sm:text-2xl font-bold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white flex items-center gap-2">
          <span>🌙</span>
          <span>Manchester City</span>
        </h2>
        <p>
          Injured, bruised, thin bench — and still running out 3-1 winners.
        </p>
        <p>
          Beth Mead with the assist to Khadija Shaw in the 86th. Mary Fowler was magnificent — orchestrating tempo, arriving in key zones, dictating the game. Yui Hasegawa and Ayaka Yamashita started on slightly unconvincing footing. Seeing Yui make an unforced error had me in disbelief; I don't think I've ever seen her give it away quite like that.
        </p>
      </section>

      <hr class="border-neutral-200 dark:border-neutral-800 my-4">

      <!-- Manchester United -->
      <section class="space-y-3">
        <h2 class="text-xl sm:text-2xl font-bold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white flex items-center gap-2">
          <span>🔴</span>
          <span>And What About Manchester United?</span>
        </h2>
        <p>
          I'm still undecided on United.
        </p>
        <p>
          I'm not convinced they played all that poorly, nor am I convinced the club vibe is as toxic as social media paints it. They lined up in a 4-2-3-1, didn't park any buses, and didn't simply play for survival. But there is undeniably plenty of potential for the usual Manchester United chaos as the season unfolds.
        </p>
        <p>
          Then again, going away to the most intriguing project in the league (London City Lionesses), in front of a sold-out crowd with a brand new manager, and with Daniëlle van de Donk playing like an absolute machine — maybe walking away with a narrow 2-1 defeat isn't the end of the world.
        </p>
      </section>
