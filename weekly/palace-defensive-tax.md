But peel back the surface, and Palace’s attacking engine is operating in virtual starvation:
    </p>

  <!-- Section 4: What Does Sports Science Say? -->
  <section class="space-y-4">
    <h2 class="text-xl sm:text-2xl font-bold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white flex items-center gap-2">
      <span class="text-emerald-500">04</span>
      <span>What Does Exercise Physiology Say?</span>
    </h2>
    <p class="text-neutral-700 dark:text-neutral-300 leading-relaxed">
      England’s description of "no oxygen in the brain" is far from hyperbole. It aligns precisely with empirical sports science on the neuromuscular and cognitive costs of acute match-play fatigue:
    </p>

    <div class="grid grid-cols-1 md:grid-cols-2 gap-4">
      <!-- Paper 1 Card -->
      <div class="p-5 rounded-xl border border-neutral-200 dark:border-neutral-800 bg-neutral-50/50 dark:bg-[#181822] space-y-3">
        <div class="flex items-center justify-between">
          <span class="text-xs font-mono font-bold text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 uppercase">PLoS ONE · Systematic Review</span>
          <span class="text-xs font-mono text-neutral-500">PMC9282585</span>
        </div>
        <h3 class="text-sm sm:text-base font-bold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white leading-snug">
          The Effect of Physical Fatigue on the Performance of Soccer Players
        </h3>
        <p class="text-xs text-neutral-600 dark:text-neutral-300 leading-relaxed">
          Dambroz et al. (2022) comprehensively demonstrated that physical fatigue accumulated during competitive soccer specifically and significantly degrades <strong class="text-neutral-900 dark:text-white">kicking accuracy, ball control, and passing precision</strong>. In acute fatigue states, technical motor skills degrade under high cardiovascular strain.
        </p>
        <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9282585/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="inline-flex items-center gap-1.5 text-xs font-mono font-bold text-indigo-600 dark:text-indigo-400 hover:underline pt-1">

  <section class="space-y-4 border-t border-neutral-200 dark:border-neutral-800 pt-6">
    <h2 class="text-xl sm:text-2xl font-bold text-neutral-900 dark:text-white flex items-center gap-2">
      <span class="text-emerald-500">05</span>
      <span>The Verdict: Protecting the Strikers</span>
    </h2>
    <p class="text-neutral-700 dark:text-neutral-300 leading-relaxed">
      Bethany England’s candid observation highlights a fundamental truth of modern tactical design: <strong class="text-neutral-900 dark:text-white">no striker can be both the primary defensive workhorse covering 60 meters backward and a clinical, cold-blooded finisher inside the box</strong>.
    </p>
    <p class="text-neutral-700 dark:text-neutral-300 leading-relaxed">
      Teams like West Ham and Aston Villa show that having 40% possession does not require sacrificing your number 9's legs. By defending as a cohesive block and releasing their forwards directly into open space on turnovers, they protect their strikers' glycogen stores and cognitive clarity.
    </p>
    <p class="text-neutral-700 dark:text-neutral-300 leading-relaxed">
      Until Crystal Palace finds a mechanism to relieve England and her attacking partners of this crushing defensive tax, their attacking metrics will remain starved, exhausted, and running on empty.
    </p>
  </section>
